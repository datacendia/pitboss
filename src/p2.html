The structural difference that matters most

In real-money gambling, the product is not what you optimise. Slot mathematics is fixed by the game supplier and certified by a test house; sportsbook prices are set by a trading team. You cannot touch either. What you can touch is everything around it: finding the product, funding the account, understanding the promotion, getting money out again, and not being frustrated into leaving.

This is unusually good news for an optimisation analyst. It means the entire conversion surface — registration, KYC, cashier, lobby, betslip, withdrawal — is fair game, and it is almost always under-optimised because the commercial teams have historically focused on bonusing rather than on funnel mechanics.

Say this

"You cannot change the maths of the game, and you would not want to. But you can change how many people get to it with money in their account, and how many of them come back. That is where I work."

Where sportsbook and casino players differ behaviourally

Sportsbook

  • Demand is event-driven and spiky — the Grand National, a derby, a title fight. Traffic can go up tenfold in an hour.
  • Extreme time pressure in-play. A two-second delay is a lost bet, not a mild annoyance.
  • Deep navigation: sport → competition → event → market → selection. Lots of places to lose people.
  • Multiples and accumulators are hugely profitable and disproportionately mobile.
  • Price changes between selection and placement cause rejections that players experience as the site "cheating" them.

Casino

  • Demand is steadier, with evening and late-night peaks and a strong weekend pattern.
  • Discovery is the core problem: hundreds or thousands of games, most of which are never launched.
  • Sessions are long, repetitive and often multi-tab.
  • Bonus mechanics (wagering requirements, game weighting, max bet rules) are the biggest source of confusion and complaint.
  • Game load performance is a direct revenue lever and is usually owned by a third party.

Cross-sell: the thing every group wants and few do well

Almost every large operator runs sportsbook and casino under one wallet and wants sportsbook customers to play casino, because casino NGR per active is typically much higher and far less volatile. The usual implementation is a banner, it usually fails, and it is a reliable early win for you: instrument the cross-sell surfaces, measure click-through and the resulting first casino session, and you will almost always find the banner is being ignored while an in-betslip or post-settlement placement performs several times better.

Part II · Module 13

The vocabulary of the room

Learn these cold. Not because you will calculate them — the finance team does that — but because using them correctly, unprompted, in the first meeting is what moves you from "agency person" to "someone who understands our business".

Revenue and margin

Handle / Turnover / Staked
The total amount wagered. Not revenue. A casino can take £100m of handle and keep £3m. Clients quote handle when they want a big number; be alert to it.
GGR
Gross Gaming Revenue
Stakes minus player winnings. The operator's gross take before any costs. GGR = Handle − Payouts
NGR
Net Gaming Revenue
GGR minus bonus cost, gaming duty, payment processing fees and often affiliate commission. This is the number that matters. When you price an opportunity, price it in NGR or you will be corrected in the meeting.
Hold % / Win %
Sportsbook. GGR ÷ Handle. Typically around 5–10% depending on mix; higher on multiples, lower on single-outcome markets and in-play.
Margin / Overround / Vig
The theoretical edge built into the prices on a market. A book priced to 105% has a 5% overround. Theoretical; hold is what actually happened.
RTP
Return to Player
Casino. The long-run percentage a game returns — typically 92–97%. House edge is 100% − RTP. Often displayed in-game for compliance reasons, which makes it something you can check is actually visible.
Bonus cost ratio
Bonus cost as a share of GGR. Operators watch this obsessively; it is frequently 20–40% and a constant tension between acquisition and finance.
Wagering requirement
How many times a bonus must be staked before it can be withdrawn — "30x". The single biggest source of player complaints and misunderstanding in the whole industry, and therefore a rich seam for UX work.
Game weighting
Which games count how much toward wagering. Slots often 100%, table games 10% or 0%. Almost never communicated clearly. Instrument whether players see it.

Players and value

FTD
First Time Depositor
A registered player who has made their first deposit. The central acquisition currency — affiliates are usually paid on it. "Reg-to-FTD" is the conversion rate everyone quotes.
Active / Actives
A player who wagered in the period. Usually "monthly actives" or "daily actives". Registration without activity is worthless.
ARPU / ARPPU
Average revenue per user; per paying user. In gambling, per active. In social gaming, ARPDAU — average revenue per daily active user — is the standard.
CPA / CAC
Cost per acquisition, usually per FTD. In competitive regulated markets this runs to the high hundreds of pounds, which is why a point of deposit conversion is worth so much.
LTV
Lifetime value, usually modelled on 6–12 month NGR. The LTV:CAC ratio is the business.
Churn / Dormancy
Churn is a defined period without activity — often 30, 60 or 90 days. Dormancy has regulatory meaning too: dormant accounts have handling rules.
VIP / High roller
A tiny population producing a very large share of revenue. Frequently 1% of players generating 40%+ of NGR. Any analysis that does not segment VIPs separately is misleading, and any VIP-affecting bug is an emergency.
Reactivation
Bringing a lapsed player back. Cheaper than acquisition and a standard CRM motion — and a good place to prove Fullstory's value, because reactivation landing experiences are usually terrible.

Operations

KYC
Know Your Customer. Identity, age and address verification. In Great Britain this must happen before a player can deposit or gamble, which puts it directly inside your conversion funnel — unlike most markets where it can be deferred.
AML / SoF / SoW
Anti-money-laundering; source of funds; source of wealth. Triggered at thresholds or by behaviour. Creates hard stops mid-journey that look like conversion failures in your funnel but are legally required. Always ask before you "fix" a drop-off.
PAM
Player Account Management platform — the system of record for accounts, wallet, bonuses and compliance. Everything else plugs into it.
PSP
Payment service provider. Operators run several and route between them; routing logic materially affects the deposit success rate you measure.
Chargeback
A disputed card payment. Costly and, at volume, threatens the operator's merchant relationships.
RG
Responsible gambling. Deposit limits, loss limits, time-outs, reality checks, self-exclusion. Both a licence condition and, increasingly, a product surface with real UX questions.
Self-exclusion / GAMSTOP
A player blocks themselves, at operator level or via the national scheme. Absolute and non-negotiable.
Affordability check
Assessing whether a player can sustain their losses. A major friction point in the GB market, often triggered mid-journey with significant conversion impact — and a place where better UX is genuinely in the player's interest.
Judgement

Some drop-offs must not be optimised away. A KYC hard stop, an affordability interruption, a self-exclusion check and a deposit limit prompt are all designed friction that the operator is legally required to impose. Your job on those journeys is to make them clearer, faster and less confusing — not to increase completion. Say this out loud, early and unprompted. It establishes that you understand the client's licence risk, and it is the fastest way to earn a compliance officer's trust.

Part II · Module 14

The lifecycle, stage by stage

Every conversation with a gambling client maps to a stage of this journey. Knowing the typical failure mode and the typical benchmark at each stage lets you diagnose an account before you have opened their Fullstory.

  1. Acquisition Affiliate link, paid search, TV-driven brand search, or app store. Heavily affiliate-dependent in most markets. Instrument landing variant, affiliate ID, campaign, device, offer shown.
    Failure mode: the offer on the affiliate page does not match the offer on the landing page. Extremely common, entirely invisible without session data, and directly measurable.
  2. Registration Typically 3–5 steps: credentials, personal details, address, marketing preferences. Benchmark 40–65% completion once started; mobile web materially worse than app.
    Failure mode: address lookup failing, password rules revealed only on submit, date-of-birth pickers that are hostile on mobile, and mandatory marketing choices presented as blockers.
  3. Verification (KYC) Automated check against credit-reference and identity databases; manual document upload on failure. Benchmark 70–90% pass automatically; the manual fallback converts far worse.
    Failure mode: the player is dropped into a document upload with no explanation of why, no indication of how long it takes, and a camera flow that fails on older Android devices. This is routinely the biggest single loss in the whole funnel and it is rarely anybody's job.
  4. First deposit The moment the acquisition spend either pays back or does not. Benchmark 50–75% of verified players deposit; card approval rates in gambling are structurally lower than in retail because issuers decline gambling MCC transactions.
    Failure mode: a decline with no useful message. The player assumes the site is broken or that they have been refused, and leaves. A decline reason mapped to a helpful next step is one of the highest-return fixes available.
  5. First play Game launch or first bet. Benchmark should be well above 90% of depositors; anything lower is a technical fault, not a preference.
    Failure mode: game fails to load, bonus terms confuse the player into hesitating, or the player cannot find the game the promotion advertised.
  6. Early retention (days 1–30) The window that sets lifetime value. Benchmark D1 return 25–40%, D7 15–25%, D30 10–18% for real-money brands.
    Failure mode: no reason to return, bonus expiry not communicated, push notifications never opted into, and a second-session experience that treats a returning player like a stranger.
  7. Established play Habitual sessions. Value now depends on frequency and session quality. Failure mode: accumulated small frictions — slow lobby, failing search, repetitive re-authentication — that individually look trivial and collectively cause drift.
  8. Withdrawal The most emotionally charged journey on the site and the biggest driver of complaints and negative reviews. Failure mode: unexplained pending states, surprise verification requirements at the moment of withdrawal, and reverse-withdrawal patterns that players experience as manipulative. Treat withdrawal UX as a retention investment, because that is exactly what it is.
  9. VIP Manual account management, personal hosts, bespoke offers. Failure mode: the digital experience is unchanged while the service promise is premium. A VIP hitting a generic error is a phone call to the CEO.
  10. Responsible gambling intervention Limits, reality checks, time-outs, or a conversation. Failure mode: tools are buried, the language is punitive, and setting a limit feels like an accusation. Genuinely better design here helps players and reduces the operator's regulatory exposure at the same time — it is the rare case where the ethical and commercial arguments align completely.
  11. Lapse and reactivation Dormancy, then a campaign. Failure mode: the reactivation link deep-links to a page requiring re-verification, or to an offer the player is not eligible for. Measure the landing experience, not just the email open rate.
  12. Closure or self-exclusion Must be easy, fast and irreversible where required. Failure mode: a closure flow with more steps than registration. Regulators look at this specifically, and so should you — it is a five-minute audit that makes you look extremely serious.
Use it

Walk this lifecycle in the first discovery workshop and ask, at each stage, "what is your number, and who owns it?" You will discover two things in under an hour: which stages nobody measures, and which stages have no owner. Those two lists are your engagement plan, and you will have built it in front of the client rather than presenting it to them.

The three questions that unblock everything

  1. "Which pages can you deploy JavaScript to today, without a third party's permission?" The honest answer defines your scope. Ask it in the kick-off, in front of the platform owner, and write the answer down.
  2. "What is the identifier that links a website session to a player record in your warehouse?" If nobody can answer immediately, that is your first workstream and everything else waits.
  3. "Who signs off a change to the cashier?" The cashier is where the money is and it is almost always the most politically contested surface on the site. Find the owner before you have findings, not after.

The rules your client lives under

You do not need to be a compliance expert. You need to know enough to avoid recommending something illegal, to recognise when a drop-off is a legal requirement rather than a defect, and to speak credibly when the compliance officer joins the call — which they will.

Things you must never recommend

  • Reducing friction in age or identity verification in a way that could let an unverified player gamble. Clarity yes; removal never.
  • Making responsible-gambling tools harder to find or use. Not even a small nudge. Not even "we moved it to a submenu for visual balance".
  • Targeting players by inferred vulnerability for any commercial purpose. If a client asks for a "high-loss-velocity" segment to send an offer to, decline and explain why.
  • Personalising promotions to self-excluded or time-out players. Ensure any segment you build feeds a suppression list, never an inclusion list, where RG status is involved.
  • Optimising the account-closure or self-exclusion flow for retention. Optimise it for speed. A regulator reading your deck should find nothing to object to.
  • Capturing anything from the KYC document journey beyond entry, outcome, reason code and duration.
Your line

"I optimise for comprehension and speed, not for completion, on any journey with a regulatory purpose. If a player is meant to stop and think, my job is to make sure they understand what they are being asked — not to get more of them through it." Learn it, mean it, and use it the first time it is relevant. It will define how the account treats you for the rest of the engagement.

Where regulation creates genuine opportunity

The constraint is also the opening. Compliance-mandated journeys are almost universally badly designed, because they were built to satisfy a legal requirement at minimum cost and nobody has revisited them since.

Make required friction legible

Players abandon KYC because nobody told them why it is happening or how long it takes. A single explanatory sentence and a progress indicator can lift completion substantially without weakening the check — the player is still verified, they just understand what is happening. This is the cleanest kind of win available on a gambling account.

Make RG tools genuinely usable

Deposit limits are typically buried in account settings behind punitive language. Presenting them neutrally and accessibly increases adoption, which reduces harm, reduces regulatory risk, and correlates with longer, healthier player lifetimes. You can argue this commercially and ethically at the same time, which is rare.

Prove compliance with evidence

Operators must show that required messaging was displayed. Fullstory can evidence that an RG message rendered, was in the viewport, and was interacted with — genuine, dated, session-level proof. Offer this to the compliance team early; it is a use case they did not know existed and it wins you an internal champion in an unexpected department.

Instrument the geolocation check

In US states, the geolocation check fails routinely — VPNs, poor GPS, desktop browsers, corporate networks. Each failure is a lost session and a support contact. Nobody measures it. Instrument attempt, outcome, reason and retry, and you will have found a meaningful loss inside a week.
