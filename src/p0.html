How this is organised

Part I — The Product

Eleven modules on Fullstory itself: the capture engine, instrumentation, identity, the event data model, privacy controls, replay craft, segments, funnels, journeys, frustration signals, warehouse export, and administration. This is the part that makes you credible in a room.

Part II — The Client's Data

What a gambling operator actually measures and why. Money mechanics, the player lifecycle, the systems landscape behind every brand, the event taxonomy you will implement, and the regulatory lines you must not cross.

Part III — Onboarding

A six-phase framework, six client archetypes with week-by-week plans, a discovery questionnaire, a role-by-role training curriculum and an account health check. Plus a generator that assembles a plan from a client profile — and flags where the profile contradicts itself — and nine copy-ready deliverable templates.

Part IV — Requirements Library

Thirty-five requirements clients actually voice, each decoded into segments, funnels, events, the KPI it moves, and the deliverable you hand back.

Part V — KPI Encyclopedia

Nine build patterns, then every metric across acquisition, casino, sportsbook, poker and bingo, social gaming, payments, KYC, retention, responsible gambling, performance and support. Open any KPI for its full build recipe: prerequisites, numbered steps, the cuts to segment by, and the trap.

Part VI — Drills

A spaced-repetition question bank in three formats, a glossary, a hands-on lab with a sandbox site containing eight planted defects, and a scenario coach that role-plays a hostile Head of Digital. Plus a sources page and how to run it all offline.

Verify

Fullstory ships changes constantly. Product facts here were checked against Fullstory's live documentation on 13 September 2026, and the Sources & confidence page lists exactly which claims were verified, which are reasoned, and which are judgement you must not present as fact. Benchmark ranges carry their own confidence tag. Before anything goes in front of a client, check the specific limit, path or parameter against the current documentation.

A note on how to use it

Do not read it front to back in one sitting. Work Part I module by module and mark each one done — your progress and your notes are saved. Then read Part II in one pass, because the vocabulary is the thing that separates an analyst who gets invited back from one who gets a written brief. Parts III to V are reference: search them in the moment.

Every module has a notes box at the bottom. Use it for the client-specific things you learn — which PSP a brand uses, who owns the tag manager, which stakeholder is hostile. That is the knowledge nobody can hand you.
