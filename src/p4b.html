The KPI encyclopedia

Every metric your clients measure, across twelve domains. Open any row for its build recipe: the pattern it follows, the instrumentation that must exist first, the numbered steps to construct it in Fullstory, the cuts to segment by, and the thing that goes wrong.

The nine build patterns are explained in the previous module. Every KPI here is tagged with the one it uses — once you know the nine, you can build any of these without looking them up.

How much to trust each range

Every KPI carries a confidence tag next to its range. Read it before you say a number out loud in a meeting. Most of these ranges are estimates; the tag tells you which are not.

published Citable

A documented standard — a web performance threshold or a card-scheme rule. Safe to state, with the source named.

industry Contextual

Widely reported across the industry and consistent with published operator reporting. Offer it as context, never as a target.

requirement Not a benchmark

A regulatory or operational requirement. The number is what the rule demands; a shortfall is a defect, not a performance gap.

client only Not comparable

No honest external comparison exists. Use the client's own trailing baseline.

estimate Smell test only — the default

My estimate from how these businesses typically behave. Use it to judge whether a client's figure is roughly normal or obviously broken. Never quote it to a client as a benchmark, and never put it in a deck as a target. Anything without another tag is one of these.

Part VI · Practice

Drill deck

Questions drawn from every part of this platform, in three formats: straight recall, spot the flaw in a real segment or instrumentation snippet, and what do you ask next when a client says something ambiguous. The explanation matters more than the score — it is the sentence you will reuse in front of a client.

How it picks

Questions are not shuffled at random. Anything you have never seen is prioritised, anything you have got wrong is weighted to return, and anything you answered correctly is held back for a while before it comes round again. Work in short sessions over several weeks rather than one long sitting — the spacing is what makes it stick.

Mastery by topic

Accuracy on the left, coverage on the right. A topic you have barely seen is not a topic you are weak at — check the seen count before you draw a conclusion.
